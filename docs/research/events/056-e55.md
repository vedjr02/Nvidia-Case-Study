# NVIDIA licenses Groq's inference technology and hires its senior leadership

- **Date:** 2025-12-24
- **Category:** corporate
- **Precision:** exact

## What happened

Groq disclosed that it had entered a non-exclusive licensing agreement with NVIDIA for its inference technology, with founder and CEO Jonathan Ross, president Sunny Madra and other senior leaders joining NVIDIA. Groq continues to operate independently. Jensen Huang told employees NVIDIA plans to integrate Groq's low-latency processors and is 'not acquiring Groq as a company'. NVIDIA issued no press release or SEC filing; CNBC reported a price of about $20 billion in cash, citing lead investor Alex Davis of Disruptive.

## Why it mattered

Structuring the transaction as a licence and talent hire rather than an acquisition avoids merger review while transferring the assets, and analysts noted antitrust as the primary risk. Groq's LPU technology was subsequently integrated into the Vera Rubin platform, indicating NVIDIA views specialised low-latency inference silicon as a gap in its own roadmap.

## Quantified impact

Reported at approximately $20 billion in cash, not confirmed by NVIDIA; Groq had raised $750 million in September 2025 at a $6.9 billion valuation, implying a large premium. NVIDIA held $60.6 billion in cash and short-term investments at end-October 2025.
