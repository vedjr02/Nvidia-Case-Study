# H20 export licence requirement imposed; $4.5 billion charge

- **Date:** 2025-04-09
- **Category:** regulatory
- **Precision:** exact

## What happened

The US government informed NVIDIA on 9 April 2025 that a licence is required for export to China, Hong Kong, Macau and D:5 countries, or to companies headquartered or ultimately parented there, of H20 integrated circuits and any circuit achieving comparable memory or interconnect bandwidth. On 14 April NVIDIA was told the requirement would be in effect indefinitely. NVIDIA initially guided to up to $5.5 billion of charges and ultimately recorded $4.5 billion in fiscal Q1 2026 for H20 excess inventory and purchase obligations.

## Why it mattered

The H20 had been designed specifically to be exportable under the 2023 thresholds, so the action established that compliant design offers no durable protection. It removed the largest remaining China revenue line and led NVIDIA to exclude China data-centre compute from guidance thereafter.

## Quantified impact

$4.5bn charge in Q1 FY2026 (initial estimate up to $5.5bn); $4.6bn of H20 sales recognised before the restriction; $2.5bn of H20 revenue unshippable in Q1; approximately $8bn of expected Q2 H20 revenue foregone; Q1 FY2026 non-GAAP gross margin 61.0% versus 71.3% excluding the charge.
