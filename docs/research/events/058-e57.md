# Commerce codifies H200 China sales with verified-buyer conditions and a 25 per cent levy

- **Date:** 2026-01-15
- **Category:** regulatory
- **Precision:** month (January 2026; Commerce rule formalising the December 2025 H200 decision)

## What happened

The Commerce Department issued a rule formalising the H200 decision, limiting sales to verified Chinese buyers and setting a 25 per cent US levy on shipments. Applicants must confirm the technology will not be used for Chinese military end uses or end users, including nuclear, missile, chemical or biological weapons purposes, must demonstrate sufficient security procedures, and must submit to inspections. Roughly ten Chinese firms including Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and JD.com were subsequently approved, along with distributors including Lenovo and Foxconn, with each approved customer permitted up to 75,000 chips.

## Why it mattered

This converted an informal political arrangement into published regulation, reducing the legal exposure NVIDIA had flagged around the 2025 revenue-share expectation. Commercially it has produced almost nothing, because Beijing has discouraged domestic firms from purchasing in order to protect its own semiconductor industry.

## Quantified impact

25% US levy on H200 China shipments; approximately 10 approved Chinese purchasers; up to 75,000 chips per approved customer under licensing terms.
