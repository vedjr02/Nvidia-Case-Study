# US agrees to licence H20 China sales in exchange for a 15 per cent revenue share

- **Date:** 2025-08-11
- **Category:** regulatory
- **Precision:** exact

## What happened

Following a meeting between Jensen Huang and President Trump, the administration agreed to grant H20 export licences in return for the US government receiving 15 per cent of NVIDIA's revenue from those sales; an equivalent arrangement was applied to AMD's MI308. Trump confirmed he had initially asked for 20 per cent. Commerce began granting licences two days after the meeting. NVIDIA's CFO subsequently stated that officials had expressed the expectation but that no regulation codifying it had been published, and NVIDIA disclosed in its SEC filing that any such revenue-share request could subject it to litigation.

## Why it mattered

The arrangement created a new and legally untested category of export cost. Legal commentators and lawmakers argued it may conflict with the Constitution's ban on export taxes and the Export Control Reform Act, leaving NVIDIA exposed to retrospective challenge, while Chinese buyers largely declined to purchase.

## Quantified impact

15% of licensed H20 China revenue payable to the US government; Bernstein estimated the arrangement could yield more than $2bn to the US government on expected sales of over $15bn (NVIDIA) and $800m (AMD) through year-end. NVIDIA reported no H20 sales made under the licences as of its Q2 FY2026 call.
