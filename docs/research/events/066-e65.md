# BIS closes the subsidiary loophole on advanced accelerator exports

- **Date:** 2026-05-31
- **Category:** regulatory
- **Precision:** exact

## What happened

BIS issued guidance clarifying that an EAR licence is required for exports of advanced computing items to entities located in any country if those entities are headquartered in Country Group D:5, including China, or Macau, or have an ultimate parent so headquartered. The guidance was prompted by industry questions about whether the November 2023 end-user licence requirement remained enforced given the May 2025 non-enforcement of the AI Diffusion Rule. Reporting indicates Chinese-parented subsidiaries incorporated in Malaysia, Singapore and the UAE had been purchasing Blackwell, Rubin and AMD MI350x-class parts without triggering controls, with industry sources estimating hundreds of thousands of processors may have moved through the gap over roughly a year.

## Why it mattered

This closes the principal remaining grey channel into Chinese-controlled compute and formally re-establishes headquarters rather than shipping destination as the licence trigger. It does not require existing data centres to stop using previously delivered chips, so the installed base remains in service.

## Quantified impact

Industry sources cited by Reuters estimated hundreds of thousands of advanced processors may have been supplied through the subsidiary channel over approximately one year.
